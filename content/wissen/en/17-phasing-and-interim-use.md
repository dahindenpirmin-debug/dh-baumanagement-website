## Why stages?

Hardly any owner can or wants to rebuild an entire site in one go. Stages spread investment and risk, keep existing buildings earning, and allow lessons from each stage to feed into the next. The precondition is a master plan ensuring that the sum of stages forms a working whole.

## How to cut stages

**1. By availability.** Which areas are free, or become free when? Lease terms set the pace.

**2. By infrastructure.** Every stage needs access, utilities, drainage, fire brigade access and parking – without temporary solutions that are expensive to replace later. Main services and the energy centre are therefore positioned so later stages can connect.

**3. By construction logistics.** Site access and compound must not disturb completed stages. Building from back to front means never driving through occupied areas.

**4. By market.** The first stage shapes the site’s image. A convincing opening with a good address makes letting all later stages easier.

**5. By financing.** Every stage has its own investment appraisal. Banks often require a pre-letting rate before releasing the construction loan.

## Handover of a stage

Each stage ends in a defined state:

- buildings accepted and let or occupied;
- external works, lighting and signage complete;
- temporary edges to future plots designed rather than merely fenced off;
- records of installed services updated;
- master plan reviewed: do the assumptions still hold?

## Interim use: income instead of vacancy

Years often lie between the last regular tenant moving out and the start of construction. Vacancy costs: heating, insurance, security, vandalism, loss of image. Interim uses turn this around.

| Suitable interim uses | What to watch |
|---|---|
| Storage, logistics, vehicle parking | floor loads, access, fire load |
| Trades, workshops, start-ups | noise, occupational safety, electrical capacity |
| Studios, culture, food and drink, events | change of use, escape routes, noise for neighbours |
| Sports and leisure | occupancy, ventilation, sanitary facilities |
| Site compound, show apartments | coordination with the construction sequence |

### Structuring the lease

- **Fixed term** with a firm end date aligned with the development schedule, with reserve.
- **Condition “as seen”** and clear rules on who bears which investments.
- **Reinstatement and vacation duty** with security.
- **Service charges** kept simple, for example as a flat rate.
- **Liability and insurance** evidenced by the user.

### Permits and safety

Clarify early with the municipality whether the interim use requires a permit and whether a time-limited permit is possible. The owner remains responsible for building safety: electrical installations, fire protection and structural safety must suffice for the new use. A short safety walk-through with a record before move-in is worthwhile.

## Do not forget the existing buildings

Buildings to be replaced in a late stage need minimum upkeep until then: watertight roofs, working drainage, safe electrics. A maintenance plan matched to the remaining period of use prevents both over-investment and costly emergencies.

## Communication

Tenants, neighbours and the municipality want to know what happens when. A simple phasing plan updated annually and a named contact build trust – and shorten later permit procedures.
