## Perspective first, measures second

Refurbishment without a use strategy is repair on suspicion. Three questions come first:

1. **Time horizon:** should the building serve another 10, 25 or 50 years?
2. **Use:** does today’s use remain, or do requirements for loads, heights, climate or tenant mix change?
3. **Economics:** what income or operational benefit stands against the investment?

## The condition survey

Component by component, the survey records condition, remaining service life and renewal cost. Typical service lives for orientation:

| Component | Service life (guide) |
|---|---|
| Structure | 80–100 years and more |
| Flat roof membrane | 25–35 years |
| Façade cladding, windows | 30–40 years |
| Heating, ventilation, cooling | 15–25 years |
| Electrical installations | 30–40 years |
| Building automation, security systems | 10–15 years |
| Floor finishes, surfaces | 10–30 years |

The survey is complemented by a structural assessment (reserves, seismic safety), a hazardous-materials survey, a review of fire protection and escape routes, and an energy assessment.

## Three strategies

**Repair.** Fix damage, secure function, maintain the standard. Sensible with a short horizon or good basic condition. Low investment, but no added value.

**Comprehensive renewal.** Envelope and services are taken into a new life cycle, layouts adapted, energy use cut significantly. The structure stays – and with it the embodied energy and often grandfathered rights under building law, for example on setbacks.

**Replacement.** When structure, height or loads do not fit, or the plot allows far more floor area. Highest investment and longest procedure, but optimal operations.

Often a combination is right: keep and renew the hall, replace or add a storey to the office wing.

## What refurbishment triggers in commercial buildings

- **Fire protection:** with changes of use or larger conversions, compartments, escape routes and systems are reassessed.
- **Seismic safety:** with substantial interventions, the proportionality of strengthening is examined.
- **Energy:** renewed components must meet cantonal energy regulations; heating replacement is subject to requirements on the share of renewable energy.
- **Hazardous materials:** removal before demolition by approved specialist firms, with proper disposal.
- **Occupational safety:** daylight, ventilation and circulation routes where workplaces change.

## Linking energy and income

Roof and façade renewal is the right moment for photovoltaics: scaffolding and waterproofing are needed anyway. Process waste heat, heat pumps and LED lighting often have short payback periods in commercial buildings. Subsidies from the Confederation, canton and utilities generally have to be applied for before work starts.

## Phasing and financing

An action plan over ten to fifteen years shows which renewals fall due when and which are sensibly bundled (for example roof, PV and lightning protection). Investments become plannable, tenants can be informed early, and value-adding portions can be separated from value-preserving ones – relevant for tax and rent setting.

## Procedure in practice

1. Condition and hazardous-materials survey.
2. Use strategy and option appraisal with rough costs.
3. Decision on strategy, budget and stages.
4. Design with trial openings in the existing fabric.
5. Execution, where necessary during live operations.
