## The process in six steps

**1. Preliminary checks.** Municipal building and zoning regulations, setbacks, building heights, density, building lines, noise, heritage and townscape inventories. In the City of Zurich and the lakeside municipalities an early conversation with the building office pays off for larger projects.

**2. Filing.** The application goes to the building office of the municipality, which also coordinates with the cantonal agencies. Filing increasingly takes place via the eBaugesucheZH platform; from 1 April 2027 it is mandatory for all applications.

**3. Profiles.** The project is marked out on site so that position and height are visible.

**4. Public notice.** The application is published and on display for 20 days, accessible via the eAuflageZH platform.

**5. Building decision.** The municipality decides together with the cantonal bodies involved and informs the owner whether anyone requested the decision during the notice period.

**6. Legal force and release.** If the decision was requested, the 30-day appeal period must be awaited. The permit conditions must then be met before construction is released.

## The Zurich particularity: request for the decision instead of objection

In most cantons neighbours object during public notice. Zurich has a two-stage system:

| Step | Deadline | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Request delivery of the building decision | 20 days from public notice | neighbours and other affected parties |
| Appeal to the Building Appeals Court | 30 days from delivery of the decision | those who requested delivery, and the owner |

For owners this has a practical consequence: after the 20 days they know whether anyone is entitled to appeal at all. If nobody requested delivery, the risk of a neighbour’s appeal falls away.

## Which procedure applies?

| Procedure | For which projects | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary procedure | new builds, larger conversions, changes of use | profiles and public notice |
| Anzeigeverfahren | smaller projects not affecting third-party interests | no profiles or notice, 30-day processing period |
| Notification procedure | certain installations such as solar systems | notification at least 30 days before work starts |

The building office decides which procedure applies in the individual case. A short enquiry before design saves time.

## Differences from the Canton of Lucerne

| Topic | Zurich | Lucerne |
|---|---|---|
| Neighbour participation | request for the decision within 20 days, then appeal | objection during public notice, generally 20 days |
| First appeal body | Building Appeals Court | under cantonal procedural law |
| Density measure | floor area, building mass or site coverage ratio, depending on the municipality | site coverage ratio |
| Electronic filing | eBaugesucheZH, mandatory from 1 April 2027 | electronic filing |

Owners building in both cantons should not transfer the terms: a Zurich request for the decision is not an objection, and density figures are calculated differently.

## What needs particular attention in Zurich

- **Dense neighbourhoods.** Attention is high in the city and along the lake. Talking to direct neighbours before profiles go up is the most effective measure against appeals.
- **Site logistics.** Narrow access, site compounds on public land and traffic arrangements need their own permits and lead time.
- **Noise and vibration.** For inner-city sites and conversions during operations, working hours and monitoring belong in the concept.
- **Inventories and protected buildings.** Many buildings are listed in inventories; clarification with the heritage authority belongs in the preliminary study.
- **Cantonal responsibilities.** Cantonal bodies are responsible for matters such as watercourses, forest, contaminated sites or occupational safety; the building office coordinates.

## Planning time realistically

On top of the procedure itself come preparing the application, the 20-day notice, the 30-day appeal period where the decision was requested, and fulfilling the conditions before release. An appeal extends the procedure by months. For a medium-sized project we recommend allowing at least six months between filing and start on site, and preparing the tender in parallel.
