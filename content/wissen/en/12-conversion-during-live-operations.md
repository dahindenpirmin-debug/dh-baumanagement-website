## Operations lead, construction follows

On a greenfield site the construction sequence sets the programme. In a live operation it is the other way round. The starting point is therefore an operational analysis with three questions:

1. **What must never fail?** Server room, cooling, compressed air, fire alarm, dispatch.
2. **What may be shut down as planned – and for how long?** A line during overhaul, a hall over the works holiday, a gate for one night.
3. **Which routes are indispensable?** Escape routes, forklift routes, lorry access, staff entrances.

## Phasing

The analysis produces a phasing plan. The decant principle has proven itself: first create replacement space (extension, temporary building, rented space), then one area moves, the vacated area is converted, the next follows. Each stage has a defined handover to operations – with working escape routes, fire protection and utilities.

For each stage, define:

- work area and separation (dust wall, hoarding, airlock);
- access for workers and materials, separate from operations;
- temporary utilities and diversions;
- restricted hours for noisy and vibration-intensive work;
- acceptance criteria before release to operations.

## Protecting people, products and plant

| Risk | Measures |
|---|---|
| Dust | dust walls, negative pressure, covering equipment, daily cleaning |
| Noise and vibration | time windows, low-vibration methods (sawing rather than breaking), monitoring at sensitive machines |
| Fire | hot-work permits, fire watch, temporary compartments, coordination with the fire alarm system |
| Accidents | separation of site and plant traffic, access control, safety induction for all contractors |
| Utility outage | tracing and recording existing services, switch-overs only in shutdown windows, fallback plan |
| Hygiene (food, pharma) | airlocks, material flow, cleaning and release process with quality assurance |

## Know the existing building

As-built drawings are rarely correct. Before tender, trial openings, service tracing, a hazardous-materials survey (a must to clarify for buildings from before 1990) and a structural assessment pay off. Every surprise found before start costs a fraction of what it would cost inside a shutdown window.

## Plan shutdown windows meticulously

For critical switch-overs – main distribution board, cooling, overhead crane, gate – an hour-by-hour script is prepared: preparation, shutdown, work, tests, restart, reserve. It includes a decision point (“continue or reinstate?”) and a fallback scenario. Materials and spare parts are complete on site before the start.

## Contracts and cost

Tender documents must state the operational constraints: working hours, stages, access rules, cleaning duties. Otherwise they become change orders. Allow a contingency of 10 to 15% and agree binding dates with liquidated damages for shutdown windows.

## Communication

A short weekly alignment between site management and plant management, a three-week look-ahead and a notice for staff work wonders. People who know when it gets loud and which entrance is closed will support the conversion.
