## The language of cost: BKP and eBKP-H

The **Baukostenplan (BKP)** is structured by trade and forms the backbone of tendering and accounts:

| BKP | Content | Typical share of total cost (excl. land) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Preparatory works (demolition, excavation, piling) | 2–8% |
| 2 | Building (structure, services, fit-out, fees) | 75–85% |
| 3 | Operating equipment | depends on operation |
| 4 | External works | 3–7% |
| 5 | Ancillary costs (charges, connections, insurance, financing) | 4–8% |
| 9 | Furnishings | as required |

BKP 0 (land) comes on top. The **eBKP-H** is structured by building element (structure, services, façade, roof, fit-out etc.). It suits early phases because elements can be priced with benchmarks before trades are known.

## Accuracy grows with design

| Phase | Instrument | Usual accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic planning, feasibility | Rough estimate from benchmarks (CHF/m² or CHF/m³) | ±20–25% |
| Concept design | Cost estimate | ±15% |
| Detailed design | Cost plan | ±10% |
| After award of main works | Revised cost plan | ±5% |

Important: the tolerance is not a contingency. A cost plan of CHF 5m at ±10% can correctly close at CHF 5.5m. If you have a hard cap, plan from the upper edge.

## Reading benchmarks correctly

Benchmarks per square metre of floor area or per cubic metre of building volume (under SIA 416) are only comparable if it is clear what they contain: BKP 2 only, or all construction costs? With or without fees, VAT, underground parking? An apartment building on a slope with piling and a basement car park can cost a third more per square metre than the same building on a flat plot.

## The most common budget traps

1. **Ground conditions.** Poor subsoil, slope water, rock or contaminated material. A geotechnical report before the cost estimate costs a few thousand francs and protects hundreds of thousands.
2. **Existing fabric.** Hazardous materials such as asbestos, inadequate structure, obsolete services. A condition and hazardous-materials survey belongs before the cost plan.
3. **Ancillary costs.** Connection charges, permit fees, construction interest and insurance are regularly underestimated.
4. **Owner changes.** Every extra wish after award is carried out at change-order prices – without competition.
5. **Inflation.** Two to three years often lie between cost plan and final account. The cost plan therefore states its price date; the Swiss Construction Price Index of the Federal Statistical Office shows the trend.
6. **Operating equipment and relocation.** Machine foundations, cranes, IT, furniture, relocation and double rent are often in no construction budget.

## Keeping the budget under control

- **Fix the cost target early** and design towards it (design-to-cost) instead of cutting at the end.
- **Monthly cost control:** cost plan, awards, change orders, forecast – on one page.
- **Manage the contingency transparently:** every draw-down is justified and approved.
- **Approve changes only with price and schedule impact.**
