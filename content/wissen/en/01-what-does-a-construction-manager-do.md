## The role in one sentence

A construction manager makes sure that drawings become a building – within the agreed budget, on the agreed date and at the quality ordered. It is where all threads meet: owner, architect, specialist engineers, authorities and contractors.

## Core tasks

**1. Plan and control costs.** The construction manager prepares the cost estimate and the detailed cost plan, structures them by the Swiss cost classification (BKP or eBKP-H) and maintains a rolling cost control with forecast during construction. The owner knows at all times where the project stands against budget.

**2. Tender and award.** For each trade, bills of quantities are prepared, offers obtained, clarified and compared. The owner receives a reasoned award recommendation and makes the award.

**3. Manage contracts.** Works contracts – usually based on SIA Standard 118 – govern price, dates, securities and warranties. The construction manager checks interim invoices, daywork sheets and change orders before payment.

**4. Plan the schedule.** From master programme to weekly plan: who works when, what must be finished first, where is the critical path?

**5. Manage the site.** Site management coordinates contractors, inspects the work, chairs site meetings and documents progress.

**6. Accept and close out.** Acceptance per trade, defects list, follow-up of rectification, final account, warranty bonds and documentation for operation.

## How the roles differ

| Role | Focus | Who contracts the trades |
|---|---|---|
| Architect | Design, permitting, often overall lead | Owner |
| Construction manager | Cost, schedule, tendering, site management | Owner |
| Owner’s representative | Represents the owner towards all parties, steers and controls | Owner |
| General / total contractor | Owes the finished building at a price and date | The contractor itself |

The key difference: the construction manager acts as a fiduciary on the owner’s side and does not earn a margin on the contractors’ work. Savings from tendering go entirely to the owner. In the general contractor model the owner buys price certainty and gives up transparency and influence in return.

## When a specialised construction manager pays off

- For **commercial and industrial buildings**, where downtime costs more than the structure itself.
- For **conversions and refurbishments**, because existing buildings hold surprises and contingencies need active management.
- For **private owners** who build once in a lifetime and want an experienced point of contact.
- For **investors** who need reliable figures for financing and returns.

## How to recognise good construction management

A cost report updated monthly. Minutes with clear actions and dates. Change orders reviewed and approved before the work is done. And a site manager who is present on site, not only on the phone. When selecting, ask for comparable references and for the person who will actually run your project.
