## Step 1: Understand the operation before drawing

The most expensive mistakes in hall construction are made in the brief, not on site. Clarify first:

- **Processes and material flow:** goods in, storage, production, picking, dispatch. Routes without crossings save money every day.
- **Loads:** floor loads (racking, machines, forklifts), crane loads, roof loads for photovoltaics and plant.
- **Clear height and grid:** rack height, crane hook, machine height; column grid matching aisles and machine layout.
- **Utilities:** power demand and connection capacity, compressed air, process heat or cooling, exhaust air, waste water.
- **Growth:** where will you extend in ten years? Gable wall, foundations and access should prepare for it.

## Step 2: Plot and constraints

Not every industrial zone is the same. Check permitted use and noise sensitivity level, heights and setbacks, lorry access (swept paths, waiting areas), ground and groundwater, entries in the register of contaminated sites, natural hazards, and capacity of power, water and sewers. The power supply is now often the critical path: transformer stations and grid reinforcement have long lead times.

## Step 3: Structure and envelope

| Decision | What matters |
|---|---|
| Structure | span, crane runway, fire resistance, construction time |
| Floor slab | point loads, flatness for high-bay or automated vehicles, joint layout, surface protection |
| Façade and roof | sandwich panels, cassette walls or timber elements; daylight via rooflights; structure for PV |
| Doors and docks | number, size, dock levellers, wind protection, canopies |
| Building services | heating (e.g. radiant ceiling panels, heat pump, waste heat recovery), ventilation by process, LED with daylight control |
| Fire protection | compartments, escape routes, smoke and heat extraction, sprinklers, fire-water retention |

Fire protection and occupational safety (daylight, view outside, escape and traffic routes) strongly shape the layout and therefore belong in concept design.

## Step 4: Set out costs realistically

On top of the building come items often missing from hall quotations: excavation support and piling, utilities and connection charges, external works with heavy-duty paving and stormwater retention, operating equipment such as cranes, racking and compressed air, machine foundations, IT and security, and the relocation. Always compare quotations from hall builders on the basis of a uniform specification.

## Step 5: Award and construction

Halls suit package awards: groundworks and slab; structure and envelope as a system; services and fit-out as separate trades. Construction of a medium-sized hall takes roughly 9 to 14 months. Steel or timber structure, doors, cranes, transformer station and air handling units must be ordered early.

## Step 6: Commissioning and relocation

Plan the machine move as a sub-project of its own: what keeps running at the old site, what moves when, where do you build stock in advance? Operating permit, crane and fire protection acceptance and staff instruction must be in place before the first production day.

## The five most common pitfalls

1. Brief too late or too vague.
2. Power connection underestimated.
3. Extension not prepared.
4. Floor slab sized by price rather than use.
5. Operating equipment and relocation missing from the budget.
