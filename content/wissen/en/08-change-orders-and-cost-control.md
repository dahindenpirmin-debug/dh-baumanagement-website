## Where change orders come from

1. **Owner changes:** an additional gate, a different floor finish, a revised layout.
2. **Gaps and errors in design and tender documents:** forgotten items, unclear interfaces between trades.
3. **Unforeseen conditions:** ground, hazardous materials, hidden damage in existing fabric.
4. **Authority requirements** after contract signature.
5. **Disruption:** obstruction, late preceding works, acceleration measures.

The first two causes can be reduced sharply: by a fully decided design before tender and by careful bills of quantities.

## The change order process in five steps

**1. Notify.** The contractor notifies additional cost in writing before doing the work. This duty belongs in every works contract.

**2. Check entitlement.** Is the work really not included? Bills of quantities, drawings, preambles and the order of precedence of contract documents are decisive.

**3. Check price.** Quantities, unit prices from the main offer, mark-ups, discounts as per contract. Omitted work is credited.

**4. Approve.** The owner decides on the basis of a recommendation with cost and schedule consequences. An authority matrix defines up to what amount site management may approve.

**5. Update.** The approved change order becomes part of the contract and of cost control. Rejected or disputed claims are tracked separately so the risk stays visible.

## Cost control on one page

A useful cost control shows for each BKP item:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cost plan | approved budget |
| Awards | works contracts signed |
| Change orders approved | released changes |
| Change orders notified | not yet decided, with risk amount |
| Still to award | open work at current estimate |
| **Forecast final cost** | sum of the above |
| Variance to budget | forecast minus cost plan |
| Payments | already paid |

The most important figure is the **forecast**, not payments to date. A project that has paid only 40% may already have exceeded its budget.

## Contingency and award savings

Award savings – when work is let below estimate – should not seep away silently. We carry them together with the contingency as a separate item. The owner then decides consciously whether the money covers extra cost elsewhere, funds additional wishes or is saved.

## Invoice checking

Interim invoices are checked against progress, not the calendar. A retention on interim payments is usual, replaced after acceptance by a warranty bond. The final invoice is only accepted after acceptance, agreed measurement and settlement of all change orders.

## What owners can contribute

- Make decisions on time and finally.
- Never order extras directly from tradespeople; go through site management.
- Ask for price **and** schedule impact with every change.
- Request and read the cost control every month.
