## Small does not mean simple

A bathroom refit, new access in commercial premises, an extension to a family home, an additional floor: the sum is manageable, the complexity is not. In existing buildings, new components meet old ones, space and time are tight, and people usually live or work next door. Fixed costs such as site set-up, scaffolding or permits also weigh more heavily in percentage terms.

## Five success factors

**1. Survey the existing building.** As-built drawings, trial openings at critical points, service routes, condition of electrics and plumbing. For buildings from before 1990, a hazardous-materials check belongs here before walls, floors or façades are opened.

**2. Finish the design, then build.** Materials, fittings, switch positions and colours are decided before tender. Every decision on site costs time and change-order prices.

**3. Honest budget.** A cost plan with 10 to 15% contingency and a clear statement of what is excluded (furniture, moving, temporary accommodation, landscaping).

**4. Few, suitable contractors.** Regional firms covering several trades suit small projects. Two or three comparable offers per main trade are enough.

**5. Present site management.** On an eight-week conversion, one lost day is over two percent of the construction time. Short lines and fast decisions are decisive.

## Typical projects at a glance

| Project | Key questions | Usual construction time |
|---|---|---|
| Interior conversion (kitchen, bathroom, layout) | load-bearing walls, services, sound insulation, habitability | 4–12 weeks |
| Extension | boundary setback, density, foundations, connection to existing | 3–6 months |
| Additional storey | structure, seismic, height, fire protection, weather protection during works | 4–8 months |
| Loft conversion | headroom, daylight, insulation, escape route | 2–4 months |
| Energy upgrade | sequence of envelope and heating, subsidies, building physics | 2–6 months |
| Commercial tenant fit-out | interface with base build, fire protection, reinstatement duty | 1–4 months |

## Do not underestimate permits

Small projects often require a permit too: changes to façade and roof, changes of use, structural interventions, new windows facing neighbours. Cantons offer simplified procedures for simple cases. Solar installations on roofs in building and agricultural zones usually only need to be notified. Ask the building office before designing – it saves weeks.

## Specifics of an additional storey

- **Structure:** an engineer checks foundations, walls and floors. With the added load, seismic safety is generally assessed as well.
- **Construction method:** prefabricated timber elements are light and erected in a few days, shortening the time the roof is open.
- **Weather protection:** temporary roof or precisely timed assembly. Water damage to the occupied floor below is the biggest risk.
- **Building services:** are heating, power supply and risers sufficient for the extra floor?
- **Access:** stairs and possibly a lift need space in the existing floors.

## Keeping costs in hand

Where possible agree lump sums for clearly defined work and unit prices for uncertain items. Daywork only on instruction and with signed sheets. A one-page cost status every two to four weeks is enough – but it must arrive.
