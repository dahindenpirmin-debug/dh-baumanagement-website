## What it is about

Whatever is built on a plot belongs to the landowner – the tradesperson cannot take back installed work. To compensate, the Swiss Civil Code (Art. 837 ff.) grants a claim to a statutory mortgage. If nobody pays, the lienholder can ultimately demand realisation of the property.

The delicate point: **subcontractors** have this right too. If the owner has paid the general contractor in full but the contractor has not paid its plasterer, the plasterer can still have a lien registered on the plot. To clear it, the owner may in the worst case pay a second time.

## The key rules

- **Deadline:** registration no later than four months after completion of the work. Minor remedial works do not extend it.
- **Provisional registration:** usually granted quickly by the court without in-depth examination. Definitive registration is then decided in ordinary proceedings.
- **The plot is affected,** regardless of who ordered the work – including tenant fit-outs the owner consented to.
- **Security instead of lien:** if the owner provides adequate security, the lien is not registered or is deleted. Since 2026 the security must cover default interest for ten years rather than indefinitely.

## When the risk is particularly high

1. Building with a **general or total contractor**, because the owner does not pay subcontractors directly.
2. **Buying off-plan** or shortly after completion, while the four-month period is running.
3. **Contractors in financial difficulty** – signalled by frequent changes of subcontractor, reminders from suppliers or pressure for advance payments.
4. **Tenant fit-outs** in commercial properties.

## Seven protective measures

1. **Check creditworthiness.** Debt enforcement register extract and references before signing.
2. **Pay by progress.** Never pay more than has been built.
3. **Demand proof of payment.** Before each interim payment, the contractor shows that subcontractors were paid for the previous stage.
4. **Agree a right of direct payment.** The owner may pay subcontractors directly and deduct the amount from the contract price.
5. **Performance bond** from a bank or insurer that expressly covers contractors’ liens.
6. **Keep a subcontractor list.** Who works when and for what amount on the plot? This is the basis for monitoring deadlines.
7. **Release the final payment only after the four-month period** or against security.

With separate trades the risk is lower because the owner pays each contractor itself. Even so, larger contractors employ subcontractors of their own.

## If a lien is filed anyway

React immediately: procedural deadlines are short. Check whether the four-month period was met, whether the claim exists and in what amount, and demand in writing that the general contractor discharge it. Engage a lawyer for the court proceedings.
