## The gap it closes

Every project has tasks nobody can take off the owner: saying what is needed, setting what it may cost, deciding in time. Then there are tasks you can delegate but should not leave to the supplier itself – checking whether the designer has delivered its full scope, or whether a contractor’s change order is justified.

Large companies have an in-house construction department for this. SMEs, foundations, cooperatives and private owners do not. The owner’s representative is their construction department for the duration of the project.

## Typical services

- **Project definition:** capture needs, draft the project brief, set budget and target dates.
- **Organisation:** define the project structure, authorities and decision paths; prepare the selection of designers and contractors.
- **Contracts:** review and negotiate consultant agreements, works contracts and general or total contractor agreements.
- **Control:** cost, schedule and quality controlling with regular reporting to the owner.
- **Decision management:** prepare decisions, show options with cost and time consequences, enforce deadlines.
- **Change management:** review project changes and change orders before they are instructed.
- **Acceptance and handover:** attend acceptance, track defect rectification, organise handover to operations.

## How it differs from construction management

The construction manager itself provides design-stage and site management services. The owner’s representative leads and checks those who provide them. On small and medium-sized projects the two roles are often deliberately combined – as overall project management with a single point of contact. On large projects and with general or total contractors, separating them makes sense.

## When does it pay off?

1. **Daily business leaves no time.** A production manager building a hall on the side decides too late or under pressure. Both cost money.
2. **The project is a one-off for the organisation.** If you build every twenty years, you know neither market prices nor the usual contract pitfalls.
3. **Many user interests compete.** Production, logistics, IT, management, board: someone has to bundle and prioritise requirements.
4. **A general or total contractor is used.** The contract defines what is delivered. What is not in it is a change order.
5. **Lenders require reporting.** Banks and investors expect independent cost and schedule reports.

## Setting up the mandate

Record in the appointment: services per SIA phase, authority limits (up to what amount may the representative approve?), reporting rhythm, fee and cap, and the person who will personally run the mandate. Independence matters: the representative must not accept commissions or interests from designers or contractors.

## What it does not replace

An owner’s representative takes work off your hands, not responsibility for fundamental decisions. Budget, standard and priorities remain with the owner. Good representatives make those decisions easier by putting the consequences in francs and weeks.
