## Why the review pays off

Buyers of existing property in Switzerland generally buy “as seen”: liability for material defects is usually largely excluded in the purchase agreement. Whatever is not identified before notarisation is borne by the buyer. A leaking 3,000 m² flat roof, a chiller with a refrigerant that is no longer permitted, or asbestos-containing floor coverings can quickly mean a six-figure sum.

## Scope of review

| Area | What is examined |
|---|---|
| Documents | building permits and conditions, drawings, land register extract with easements, maintenance contracts, test records, leases with fit-out and reinstatement duties |
| Structure | condition, cracks, corrosion, imposed loads, seismic safety, reserves for conversion |
| Envelope | roof, façade, windows, doors, waterproofing, thermal insulation |
| Building services | heating, ventilation, cooling, plumbing, electrical, automation, lifts – age, condition, capacity, spare-part availability |
| Fire protection | conformity with permit and use, escape routes, compartments, maintenance of systems |
| Hazardous materials and environment | asbestos, PCBs, PAHs; entry in the register of contaminated sites; tanks |
| Legal compliance | does actual use match permitted use? Have conditions been met? |
| Energy | consumption, heating system, upcoming legal requirements, PV potential |
| Potential | unused development rights, options for extra storeys and extensions |

## Process

1. **Review the data room** and draw up a question list for the seller.
2. **Inspect** all areas including roof, plant rooms and basements, with photo record.
3. **Talk to the caretaker or operator** – often the richest source.
4. **Evaluate:** condition per component, remaining life, measures and cost.
5. **Report** with summary, traffic-light rating and investment plan.

## The investment plan

The core of the report is a table assigning each measure to a period and priority:

- **Immediate (0–1 year):** safety, authority requirements, acute damage.
- **Short term (1–3 years):** deferred maintenance, components at end of life.
- **Medium term (4–10 years):** plannable renewals.

The total of immediate and short-term measures is the basis for negotiating with the seller.

## Typical findings in commercial and industrial buildings

- Flat roofs at end of life, often with saturated insulation.
- Unpermitted installations and changes of use (mezzanines, offices in warehouses).
- Fire protection deficiencies from later service penetrations and obstructed escape routes.
- Fossil-fuel heating whose replacement requires renewable solutions.
- Floor slabs with cracks or insufficient capacity for the intended use.
- Ground contamination from earlier uses such as filling stations, electroplating or chemical stores.
- Tenant fit-outs where reinstatement duty and ownership are unclear.

## After the report

Depending on findings, further investigations (hazardous materials, ground, structure), price negotiation, representations in the purchase agreement or a retention in escrow are advisable. For contaminated sites, note also that the sale or subdivision of a registered plot may require official approval.
