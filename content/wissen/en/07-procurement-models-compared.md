## The three basic models

**Separate trade contracts (Einzelleistungsträger).** The owner appoints the architect, engineers and each contractor individually. The construction manager coordinates and administers the contracts on the owner’s behalf.

**General contractor (GU).** Design stays with the owner’s designers. The GU takes on all construction under a single works contract and sublets the work.

**Total contractor (TU).** The TU takes on design and construction. The basis is a brief or a concept design with specification.

## Comparison

| Criterion | Separate trades | GU | TU |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contract partners | many | one for construction | one for everything |
| Price certainty | grows with awards | high from contract | high from contract, early |
| Price transparency | full | depends on price type | low to medium |
| Influence on choice of firms | full | limited | low |
| Flexibility for changes | high | medium, via change orders | low, via change orders |
| Coordination risk | with owner / construction manager | with GU | with TU |
| Owner’s effort | higher | medium | lower, but control needed |
| Suited to | conversions, high-quality buildings, experienced owners | clearly defined new builds | standard buildings, tight dates, investors |

## Price types

- **Lump sum (Pauschalpreis / Globalpreis):** a fixed price for a defined scope. The Globalpreis is index-linked, the Pauschalpreis is not.
- **Guaranteed maximum price, open book:** ceiling price with insight into all costs and a share of savings.
- **Unit prices:** payment by measured quantities, usual for separate trades.

## The decisive question: how finished is the design?

A lump sum is only as good as the specification. Whatever is undefined at signing later becomes a change order or a quality dispute. Therefore:

- **Design largely defined, few changes expected:** GU or TU plays to its strengths.
- **Conversion of existing fabric, many unknowns:** separate trades with a properly managed contingency are usually more honest and cheaper, because the contractor need not price incalculable risk.
- **Owner wants to shape the result:** separate trades, or GU with guaranteed maximum price and open book.
- **Maximum relief and an early fixed price:** TU – with an independent owner’s representative as counterweight.

## What to check in a GU/TU contract

1. **Specification with qualities and allowances** – products or equivalent standards, not just catchwords.
2. **Change procedure:** deadlines, pricing, schedule effects.
3. **Securities:** performance bond, warranty bond, protection against subcontractors’ liens.
4. **Payment schedule by progress**, not by calendar.
5. **Dates with liquidated damages** and a clear definition of readiness for occupation.
6. **Defect rights:** since 2026 the right to rectification for buildings can no longer be excluded; assigning defect rights to the owner does not replace the contractor’s own liability.

## Hybrid forms

Combinations work well in practice: structure and envelope as one package, fit-out as separate trades; or a GU for the new build and separate trades for converting the existing building. What matters is that the interfaces between packages are clearly described.
