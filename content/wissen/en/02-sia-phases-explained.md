## Why phases?

Building is a sequence of decisions that become ever more expensive to correct. Moving a wall at concept stage costs a pen stroke; on site it costs weeks. The phase model of the Swiss Society of Engineers and Architects (SIA 112) therefore creates clear stopping points: each sub-phase ends with a defined deliverable, and the owner decides whether and how to proceed.

## The six phases at a glance

| Phase | Sub-phases | Deliverable | Typical cost accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Strategic planning | 11 Statement of needs, solution strategies | Needs, goals, constraints | Budget frame, rough |
| 2 Preliminary studies | 21 Feasibility study, 22 Selection procedure | Feasibility, project brief | approx. ±20–25% |
| 3 Design | 31 Concept design, 32 Detailed design, 33 Permit procedure | Permitted project | ±15% (concept), ±10% (detailed) |
| 4 Tendering | 41 Tender, bid comparison, award recommendation | Works contracts | Market prices |
| 5 Construction | 51 Construction documents, 52 Execution, 53 Commissioning, close-out | Accepted building | Final account |
| 6 Operation | 61 Operation, 62 Maintenance | Operation and upkeep | – |

## What really matters in each phase

**Phases 1 and 2 – the cheapest time for good decisions.** This is where you establish whether to build at all, what the plot allows and what the project may cost. A sound project brief (space programme, quality, budget, dates) is the best insurance against later change orders.

**Phase 3 – from concept to permitted project.** Options are tested in concept design; the chosen solution is worked through in detailed design: structure, building services, materials, cost plan. The permit application follows.

**Phase 4 – the market confirms the cost.** Bills of quantities are prepared and offers obtained. If the bid comparison deviates from the cost plan, this is the moment to react: adjust scope, test alternatives, reassess contingencies.

**Phase 5 – build, control, hand over.** Construction drawings, site, cost control, acceptance and final account. Owner changes are most expensive here and should only be released against a priced change order.

**Phase 6 – the building in use.** Warranty works, maintenance planning and renewal. Thinking about operating costs in phase 3 often saves more over the life cycle than on the construction cost.

## Typical mistakes

- **Skipping phases:** going straight to detailed design without a feasibility study – and discovering months later that budget or zoning do not fit.
- **Postponing decisions:** open material choices drift into construction and become schedule problems.
- **Unclear division of services:** if it is not clear who delivers which sub-phase, gaps appear – for example in the coordination of building services.

## Our tip

Ask for a short dossier at the end of each sub-phase: project status, cost, schedule, risks, pending decisions. A formal phase gate takes one meeting and prevents a project from rolling on without approval.
