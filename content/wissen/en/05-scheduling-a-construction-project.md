## Project duration is more than construction time

When owners talk about the date, they mean moving in or starting production. The road there consists of five blocks – and the site is only the last one.

| Block | Small project (up to approx. CHF 1m) | Medium project (CHF 1–10m) | Large project (over CHF 10m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preliminary study, feasibility | 1 month | 2 months | 4 months |
| Design | 3 months | 5 months | 9 months |
| Permit | 3 months | 4 months | 6 months |
| Tendering, award | 2 months | 3 months | 5 months |
| Construction | 6 months | 14 months | 24 months |
| **Total** | **approx. 15 months** | **approx. 28 months** | **approx. 48 months** |

These are benchmarks without objections and without special land-use planning. Phases can partly overlap, for example tendering during the permit procedure.

## Three levels of planning

1. **Master programme:** one page, all phases, the owner’s milestones (project approval, funding decision, permit filing, start on site, occupation).
2. **Construction programme:** all trades with dependencies, lead times and critical path.
3. **Detailed programmes:** weekly or stage programmes for sensitive sections, such as a conversion during a works shutdown.

## What really endangers the schedule

**Late decisions.** Kitchen, floor finishes, machine layout, tenant fit-out: every decision has a latest date after which it holds up the works. A decision schedule with deadlines is the owner’s most effective scheduling tool.

**Lead times.** Lifts, windows, air handling units, transformer stations, steelwork or fire doors have lead times of several months. These trades must be tendered and awarded early – if necessary ahead of the rest.

**Utilities and third parties.** Power connection, water, district heating and road works lie with utilities and the municipality. Their deadlines are in no works contract, yet often determine occupation.

**Weather and drying.** Concreting in winter, screeds before parquet: physics is not negotiable. Realistic programmes include drying times instead of buying them at the end with dehumidifiers.

**Commissioning.** In technically demanding buildings, integrated tests of fire protection, ventilation and building automation take several weeks. They belong before occupation, not after.

## Securing dates by contract

Works contracts should fix intermediate and completion dates. Liquidated damages only work if dates are clearly defined and the owner has met its own obligations – drawings, decisions, preceding works – on time. Record delays promptly in the site meeting minutes.

## Our approach

We plan backwards from the move-in date, show the float openly and keep a decision schedule for the owner. Every two to four weeks the plan is compared with actual progress. That way a delay becomes visible while it can still be recovered.
