## The process in six steps

**1. Preliminary checks.** Before drawings are made: zone, building and zoning regulations, setbacks, heights, site coverage ratio, watercourse corridors, hazard map, register of contaminated sites. For larger projects a preliminary enquiry with the municipality is worthwhile – it settles sensitive points before design costs arise.

**2. Prepare the application.** It comprises forms, the surveyor’s site plan, floor plans, sections and elevations, landscape plan, energy and fire protection verifications and, depending on the project, further documents (noise, drainage, parking, shelter). In the Canton of Lucerne applications are filed electronically.

**3. Completeness check and profiles.** The municipality checks completeness. The building outline is marked on site with profile poles (Baugespann) so neighbours can see its dimensions.

**4. Public notice.** The application is generally on display for 20 days. Affected parties may object within this period.

**5. Review and coordination.** The municipality assesses the application; where cantonal permits or opinions are needed, they are obtained in a coordinated procedure.

**6. Decision.** The permit contains conditions – read them carefully. Once the appeal period has expired the permit is final, and work may start after the formal release.

## Specifics for commercial and industrial projects

- **Occupational safety:** under the Swiss Labour Act, industrial operations need plan approval and, before start-up, an operating permit. Plans are also reviewed for non-industrial operations with particular hazards.
- **Environment:** noise verification, air quality, water protection, storage of water-polluting substances, and for large facilities an environmental impact assessment or major-accident prevention.
- **Fire protection:** a fire protection concept under the VKF regulations, for large fire compartments often with sprinklers or smoke and heat extraction.
- **Traffic and access:** lorry access, parking, possibly a traffic report.
- **Contaminated sites:** if the plot is listed in the register, an assessment under contaminated-sites law is needed before building.

## The most common causes of delay

1. **Incomplete documents.** Every request for additional information costs weeks.
2. **Objections from neighbours.** Talking to neighbours before the public notice is the cheapest measure in the whole project.
3. **Exemptions.** Pushing setbacks or heights to the limit and needing exemptions lengthens the procedure and raises the risk of objections.
4. **Design changes after filing.** Material changes require a new public notice.
5. **Issues discovered late.** Watercourse corridors, natural hazards, townscape protection or contamination belong in the preliminary checks, not in the permit procedure.

## Scheduling it properly

Allow not only for the procedure itself but also for preparing the application (one to three months depending on the project) and the time between permit and start on site: fulfilling conditions, construction documents, tendering. Tendering can run in parallel with the procedure – but award only once the permit is final, or include a corresponding reservation in the contract.

During construction the municipality requires inspections, typically at setting-out, at shell completion and before occupation. These dates belong in the construction programme.
