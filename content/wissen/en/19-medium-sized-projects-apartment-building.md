## The order of magnitude

Medium-sized projects – typically an apartment building with 6 to 40 units or a smaller commercial building – sit between the clarity of a conversion and the organisation of a large project. They need a full design team, structured cost and schedule management and usually external financing, but no project bureaucracy of their own.

## 1. Economics first

Before the first line is drawn comes the calculation:

- **Total investment:** land, construction, ancillary costs, financing during construction, marketing.
- **Income:** achievable net usable area times market rent or sale price.
- **Key figures:** gross yield or development profit, cost per square metre of usable area, area efficiency (usable area in relation to gross floor area).

Area efficiency is the underestimated lever: a compact volume with efficient circulation yields more lettable area per franc invested than any saving on finishes.

## 2. The right product

Unit mix, sizes and fit-out standard follow location and target group. Decisions with major cost impact are the number of basements and parking spaces, the façade, the number of stair cores and lifts, and the energy standard. An underground car park can account for a double-digit percentage of building cost depending on the ground.

## 3. Project organisation

| Role | Task |
|---|---|
| Owner | goals, budget, decisions |
| Overall project manager / owner’s representative | steering, reporting, preparing decisions |
| Architect | design, permit, construction documents |
| Construction manager | cost, tendering, site management |
| Engineers | structure, building services, building physics, fire protection, geology |
| Marketing | positioning, sales or first letting |

One party must coordinate all engineers, especially building services: risers, penetrations and plant rooms are the most common source of clashes.

## 4. Cost and procurement

At this scale both separate trades and a general contractor are common. With separate trades the owner keeps influence on quality and choice of firms and benefits from award savings; the general contractor offers an early fixed price. For condominiums with many buyer options, the flexibility of separate trades is often an advantage.

The cost plan is revised after awarding groundworks and structure, windows, building services and façade – by then around two-thirds of the cost is secured by contract.

## 5. Time frame

| Phase | Duration |
|---|---|
| Feasibility, concept design | 3–4 months |
| Detailed design, permit application | 3–4 months |
| Permit | 3–5 months |
| Construction documents, tendering | 3–4 months, partly in parallel |
| Structure | 6–8 months |
| Fit-out, external works | 8–10 months |

## 6. Marketing in parallel with construction

Sales or letting documentation is prepared with the detailed design; marketing usually starts at ground-breaking. For condominiums, banks often require a pre-sale rate. A show apartment, sample selection days and a clear buyer’s manual save many individual meetings later.

## 7. Acceptance and handover

First the works are accepted from the contractors, then the apartments are handed over individually to buyers or tenants – with a record, key receipt and instruction. The owner receives complete as-built documentation; for condominiums part of it goes to the property manager. Plan fixed collective dates for defect rectification after occupation rather than individual call-outs.

## Typical mistakes

- Financial appraisal done only after concept design.
- Basement and excavation underestimated.
- Buyer options without cut-off dates.
- External works treated as a leftover – they shape the first impression and are built last, when the budget is spent.
